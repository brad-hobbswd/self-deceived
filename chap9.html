The Freedom Found in Doing the Word

SELF-DECEIVED · When Religious Knowledge Replaces Obedience

People sometimes hear the word obedience and imagine a life where every desire is denied and every choice feels like a restriction. Yet Scripture describes a freedom that goes deeper than getting our own way. God does not call us to be ruled by habits, reactions, or desires that keep taking us somewhere we do not want to go. His truth leads us toward a life with greater honesty, wisdom, and peace.

Jesus said in John 8:31–32 that those who continue in His Word are truly His disciples, know the truth, and are made free. He was speaking about freedom from sin. Notice that He connects freedom with continuing. We do not hear one truth, have one powerful moment, and then no longer need instruction. We keep walking in the Word because our lives are continually being shaped by what we receive.

Consider someone who has carried resentment for years. Every conversation about the person who hurt them turns into the same story. Their choices, mood, and relationships are affected by the offense. Forgiveness may not mean pretending the wrong never happened, excusing abuse, or removing wise boundaries. Yet obedience can begin freeing the heart from the need to rehearse the pain constantly or let the offender continue controlling every response. Healing may take time, support, and prayer, but the direction matters.

Other patterns can hold us just as tightly. We may depend upon approval before making any decision. We may lie to protect an image, react impulsively when frustrated, or keep returning to a habit that leaves us ashamed. The Word can interrupt those patterns. Sometimes obedience looks like saying no, asking for help, making a difficult change, or choosing not to send the message we have written in anger. Freedom is not always dramatic. Sometimes it is a quiet choice made differently today.

Truth also frees us from pretending we are perfect. We do not have to spend our lives defending every mistake or managing everyone's opinion of us. We can acknowledge weakness, receive correction, and seek mercy. Humility is not humiliation. We can face the truth about ourselves without believing that failure is the only thing God sees. Grace gives us a way to get up, take responsibility, and continue growing.

Galatians 1:10 challenges the desire to live for human approval instead of Christ. We cannot always make everybody understand our decisions. A faithful choice may disappoint someone, invite criticism, or be misunderstood. Approval becomes a kind of bondage when we change our convictions just to keep people pleased. Our allegiance must be settled in God, even while we treat others with compassion and respect.

Romans 12:2 speaks of transformation through the renewing of our minds. As Scripture changes the way we think, we begin recognizing what is good and learning to choose it. Obedience does not earn God's love, and we do not change through our strength alone. Grace forgives us, strengthens us, and helps us keep moving. The freedom of doing the Word is not freedom from God's authority. It is freedom to live in the wisdom of the One who gives life. The question is whether we will keep walking in that freedom.

The Freedom Found in Doing the Word

SELF-DECEIVED · When Religious Knowledge Replaces Obedience

Obedience is sometimes imagined as a life narrowed by restrictions, a constant surrender of desire without benefit beyond compliance. Scripture presents a richer picture. God’s truth leads us away from patterns that diminish life and toward freedom grounded in wisdom, integrity, and relationship with Him.

Jesus said in John 8:31–32 that those who continue in His Word are truly His disciples and will know the truth, and the truth will make them free. Continuing matters. Freedom is not a moment of exposure followed by independence from instruction. The disciple remains in the Word, allowing truth to shape understanding and choices.

Jesus speaks in that passage about bondage to sin. Repeated choices can form habits that become difficult to interrupt. The Word exposes those patterns and gives direction for a different way. Obedience may require discipline and uncomfortable decisions, yet the purpose is not to leave us trapped in shame. God calls us toward a life no longer controlled by what harms us.

Freedom becomes visible when resentment no longer dictates every response, approval no longer governs decisions, dishonesty no longer protects our image, and destructive impulses are met with restraint. Temptation may remain, and growth may take time, but our struggles do not have to determine the direction of our lives. We can seek God, receive help, practice new choices, and continue growing.

Truth also frees us from pretending. We do not have to defend every mistake or manipulate circumstances to appear right. Living honestly before God allows us to acknowledge weakness and receive correction without making our identity depend upon flawless performance. Humility opens the way for growth.

Galatians 1:10 challenges the desire to live for human approval rather than Christ. Approval becomes bondage when others’ expectations govern our convictions. Obedience may invite misunderstanding or disappoint people. Freedom grows as allegiance becomes settled in God rather than in changing opinions.

Romans 12:2 connects transformation with a renewed mind. As the Word reshapes our thinking, we learn to recognize what is good and pleasing to God. A renewed mind does not merely know the right answer; it learns to value what is right and choose with increasing consistency.

Obedience does not earn God’s love, and freedom does not remove our need for grace. Grace enables response and sustains growth. The Word gives direction, and God’s grace meets us as we walk in that direction. Doing the Word is not freedom from God’s authority, but freedom to live within the wisdom of His authority, increasingly aligned with the One who gives life.

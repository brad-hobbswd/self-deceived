Preface

There is a danger in becoming familiar with the Word of God while remaining unwilling to submit to what the Word requires. Familiarity can make truth feel comfortable. We may hear Scripture so often that the message becomes recognizable without allowing the message to search our conduct, correct our attitudes, or change our decisions.

James 1:22 gives a direct warning: believers must be doers of the Word, not hearers only, deceiving themselves. Self-deception may not feel like deception. A person can believe that spiritual knowledge, good intentions, religious activity, or past experiences demonstrate maturity while overlooking the need for present obedience.

This book is not written as an accusation against other people. The subject requires personal examination. Each of us must allow Scripture to reveal where understanding has outpaced willingness, where conviction has been acknowledged without change, and where explanations have become a way of avoiding responsibility.

The chapters explore hearing, understanding, conviction, repentance, conduct, integrity, and obedience. Scripture remains the foundation. The goal is not perfection through human effort, but a life that responds to truth with humility and willingness.

There is a difference between struggling to obey and refusing to obey. Struggle may bring a person to prayer, repentance, accountability, and renewed effort. Refusal protects the behavior, resists correction, or repeatedly dismisses what has been made clear.

May we never become so familiar with the truth that we lose our willingness to obey it.

Bradley Hobbs
